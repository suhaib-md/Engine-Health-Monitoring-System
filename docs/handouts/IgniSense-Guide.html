Chapter 1

What IgniSense is

A digital twin of a 2.0 L four-cylinder petrol engine that runs entirely in the browser. It shows how a monitoring system can tell that an engine is failing, which part is failing, and how long is left, using only sensor readings.

The idea in one paragraph

Most maintenance is done on a fixed schedule: it either wastes parts or misses failures. Condition monitoring watches the engine instead. IgniSense simulates two engines side by side. The Plant is the "real" engine, and it can develop hidden faults such as a wearing oil pump. The Twin is a healthy copy of the same physics that only sees the sensor readings and predicts what every sensor should read. The Analytics compare the two. The gap between what the sensors read and what the healthy Twin expects is the evidence, and everything else (fault name, severity, health score, remaining life) is built from that gap.

Chapter 2

Run it on your computer

There is no server, no account and no internet needed once it is installed. Everything runs in the browser on your own machine.

What you need

  • Node.js 20.19 or newer, the LTS version from nodejs.org. Check it by opening a terminal and typing node --version.
  • A recent Chrome, Edge or Firefox. Web Serial (ESP32 / OBD-II) works in Chrome and Edge only.
  • Internet, once, for the first install. After that it runs offline.

Easiest way: double-click

  1. Download or copy the project folder to the computer and unzip it.
  2. On Windows, double-click start-ignisense.bat. On macOS or Linux, open a terminal in the folder and run sh start-ignisense.sh.
  3. The first run installs the dependencies. Keep the window open until it finishes (a minute or two, needs internet). Later runs skip this and take a few seconds.
  4. It builds the app and opens http://localhost:4173 in your browser.
  5. To stop it, close the window (or press Ctrl+C).

4.2  Injecting a fault

A fault is applied to the hidden Plant. Nothing on screen tells the monitor which one you chose: it has to find it in the sensors.

  1. Start the engine (Start engine) and let it warm up. Set time-warp to 60× to speed this up. Wait until the badge says RUNNING · STEADY.
  2. Open the Test bench. Under Fault injection, choose a Fault from the list.
  3. Set the Severity (0 to 1). Higher is worse. The small note underneath tells you the resulting health factor (for example severity 0.60 leaves oil-pump health at 0.55).
  4. Choose the Onset: GRADUAL ramps in over 120 simulated seconds, INSTANT is immediate, PROGRESSIVE keeps the fault growing (faster under load, heat and speed) so the remaining-life estimate has a trend to follow.
  5. Click Inject fault. The drawer closes. Watch the Live Twin page.
  6. To clear it, open the bench and click Repair. Alerts clear after their hold time (about 20 simulated seconds per level).
FaultWhat it does to the engineWhat the monitor sees
Oil-pump wearPump health = 1 − 0.75 × severity. Oil pressure drops, even at idle.Pressure far below the Twin's expectation; lubrication subsystem falls; CUSUM warns early.
Cooling degradationRadiator loses capacity (health = 1 − 0.8 × severity). Coolant runs hot under load.Coolant residual and its rate of change; thermal subsystem falls.
Cylinder misfireChoose C1–C4. That cylinder's combustion health = 1 − severity (1 = dead cylinder).Half-order line in the crank-speed spectrum; the polar plot names the cylinder.

4.4  Live Twin page

The 3D engine

  • Built from code, not a downloaded model. The piston positions come from the slider-crank equation, so TDC and BDC line up with crank angle 0° and 180°.
  • It is slowed 100× so you can see the motion. The label DISPLAY SLOWED 100× and the readout engine 3,000 rpm · shown at 30 rpm say so honestly.
  • Cylinders flash in firing order 1-3-4-2. A cylinder that the monitor has diagnosed as misfiring stops flashing. The block shakes at the real second-order vibration.
  • The block turns from dark to cyan as the coolant warms. The oil gallery glows with pressure. A faulty subsystem's parts pulse in the status colour.

Steps:

  1. Click FRONT, CUTAWAY, TOP or EXPLODE (top-left of the view) for a camera preset. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom.
  2. Click a diamond marker (oil pump, radiator) or hover it: a label slides out with that part's health.
  3. Click the label to open a side panel with that part's sensors: measured, expected and residual. View calculation opens the equation for it.
  4. Read the frame rate at the top of the view (60 fps · 71 meshes). reduced effects means it lowered its own quality to stay smooth.

The health ring and subsystems

The ring shows overall health from 0 to 100, coloured by the overall alert level (not by score bands). Under the number: the engine state or the alert class, and "limited by …" naming the subsystem that drives a WARNING or CRITICAL level (the overall number is a weighted average, so it can look mild beside one failing subsystem), the name of the diagnosed fault ("No fault evidence" when healthy), and a remaining-life line when a significant trend exists (otherwise it says what the statistics layer is doing, for example "learning the healthy baseline").

Below are six subsystems, each 0 to 100, with their weights: Lubrication 0.25, Thermal 0.25, Mechanical vibration 0.20, Combustion 0.15, Electrical 0.10, Sensor integrity 0.05. A dash means it is not monitored while the engine is off.

Signals: the gauges

  • Engine speed is a car-style tachometer (0 to 7 in thousands of rpm, redline from 6,000). The white pointer on the rim is the speed the Twin expects.
  • Engine load, Coolant, Oil temp, Oil pressure, Voltage, Vibration RMS are bar gauges. The white tick and TWIN value show what a healthy engine would read right now, at the same speed, load and ambient.
  • Δ is the residual (measured − expected). It turns coloured once it leaves the normal noise band (more than 2 σ).
  • The coloured strip under a bar marks warning and critical zones. The small square in the corner is the sensor quality: green valid, amber degraded, red failed, grey unavailable.
  • A dead sensor shows — — with a hatched track. It is never shown as 0.

Steps: click any gauge's number, or its ƒ(x) mark, to open its calculation (4.7).

Diagnosis card and alerts

  • Probable fault with a severity badge and an evidence score. It is deliberately called a "diagnostic evidence score", never "confidence %". Classes: under 0.30 weak, 0.30–0.55 possible, 0.55–0.75 probable, over 0.75 strong.
  • Why the system thinks this is a checklist of named symptoms, each with its status colour.
  • Recommended action, then Model status (physics rules, anomaly model, remaining life) and Confidence in this output (evidence strength, data quality, model agreement, uncertainty).
  • Alerts are INFO, WATCH, WARNING or CRITICAL. Each carries the time, the subsystem, measured vs expected, the residual, how long it has persisted and whether a rule or a statistic raised it.

Cause and effect graph

At the bottom of the page a graph links each fault the monitor can name to its physical effect and to the sensor that sees it. Nodes light up from the live symptom risks, so you can trace a reading back to its cause while the evidence builds.

4.5  Trends

Charts of measured against expected over simulated time (they scroll as the clock runs).

  • Charts: oil pressure, coolant, oil temperature, bus voltage, and overall health. The solid line is measured, the dashed line is the Twin, the shaded band is the residual.
  • Markers: violet vertical lines are fault injections; diamonds are alerts.
  • Statistics: D² (Mahalanobis distance) flags an unusual combination of residuals against its 99 % limit (15.09). CUSUM accumulates small persistent shifts and crosses its limit of 5 well before a fixed threshold trips.

Steps: inject a fault (4.2) and open Trends. Watch CUSUM on oil pressure cross 5 before the rule-based alert appears: that is the early warning.

4.6  Vibration and misfire

  • Metrics: vibration RMS, peak, crest factor, kurtosis, and the crank-speed irregularity.
  • Order spectrum: vibration strength against engine order (multiples of crank speed), with cursors at 0.5×, 1× and 2×. A four-cylinder engine shakes at 2×. A misfire adds a 0.5× line.
  • Misfire polar plot: a dot at the phase of the 0.5× component. It falls in the sector of the misfiring cylinder: C1 +45°, C2 +135°, C3 −45°, C4 −135°. The size of the dot tells how much torque is missing.
  • Waveforms: the accelerometer signal and the crank-speed ripple over 16 revolutions.
  • Device accelerometer: a panel that runs a phone's motion sensor through the same FFT (needs a phone, https or localhost).

Steps:

  1. Run the engine at about 3,000 rpm. Open Vibration and note the clean 2× line.
  2. Test bench, Fault: Cylinder misfire, Cylinder: C3, click Inject fault.
  3. Wait a few seconds. A 0.5× line appears, the polar dot jumps to the C3 sector (−45°), and the diagnosis card names Cylinder 3 misfire.
  4. Click Sound on (Live Twin): one exhaust pulse in four goes missing, and the engine audibly stumbles.

4.7  Calculations

The app can show its own working. Every equation is registered with its symbols, units and a template, so the calculation view can substitute today's live numbers into it.

  1. On Live Twin, click a gauge's number (for example oil pressure). A drawer opens from the right.
  2. Read the chain of equations. Numbers are colour-tagged: measured, Twin state, healthy assumption, inferred, or constant.
  3. Look at the checks against the screen table at the bottom. Each row shows the equation's result beside the gauge value and a ✓ EQUAL.
  4. Flip the switch at the top (Live · updates 20× a second) to Holding these numbers. It freezes the numbers so you can read them out while the engine runs.
  5. For the full list, open the Math page: pick a gauge to see its live chain, and scroll for all 28 registered equations with their symbols and units.
SayEvery number on this screen has an equation behind it, and you can click it. We test that the numbers substituted into the equation equal the gauge on the screen, in six different engine states.

4.8  Blind test

Proof that the monitor is not reading the injected fault. A hidden fault is chosen inside the simulation worker; the page only ever learns the card's position, never its content.

  1. Open the Test bench and click Start blind test. The drawer closes and the Live Twin page shows nine sealed cases.
  2. Ask a visitor to select a case. Nobody, including you, knows what is on it.
  3. The engine runs at part load and 10× warp. Watch the diagnosis card: within seconds to about a minute the monitor names a fault.
  4. Press Reveal the card. The strip shows what was on the card and whether the monitor was ✓ CORRECT.
  5. Click New blind test for another round.

The nine cards are: oil-pump wear, cooling degradation, a misfire in each of the four cylinders, worn crank bearings, an alternator losing output, and a stuck coolant sensor. Every card is named correctly in the tests.

4.9  Validation

The Validation page computes the physics live in your browser and compares each result with a value worked out by hand. Examples: angular speed 314.16 rad/s, F₂ = 2,517 N at 3,000 rpm, cold-start oil pressure 2.54 bar, the 4/3 misfire torque ratio, the χ² limit 15.09, and the health example 69.4. All rows should read PASS. It is your answer to "how do you know the model is right?"

4.10  Maintenance report

  1. Run a fault so there is something to report.
  2. Open Report. It shows "IgniSense maintenance report · Team Revora": the verdict, the evidence, the recommended action, the remaining-life band, subsystem bars, health history, model status and confidence, exposure counters and recent alerts.
  3. Click Refresh from engine to take a new snapshot (it does not update by itself).
  4. Click Print / save PDF. Choose "Save as PDF" as the printer. It prints on exactly one A4 page and the file is named ignisense-report-<time>.

4.11  Engine sound

The sound is built from what the monitor measures, not from the hidden fault. Each cylinder fires an exhaust pressure pulse as loud as its measured power stroke, in the order 1-3-4-2, at the real engine speed (the 3D view is slowed, the sound is not). The pulses pass through a small model of an exhaust system (a pipe resonance, a muffler boost, and saturation that gets rougher under load).

  1. On Live Twin click Sound off (it becomes Sound on). The click is required by the browser.
  2. Start the engine. Change the target RPM and load: pitch follows speed smoothly, and it gets louder and brighter under load.
  3. Inject a cylinder misfire: one pulse in four disappears and the engine stumbles.

Chapter 5

How the engine simulation works

Two clocks run inside a Web Worker: a slow physics loop for temperatures and pressure, and a fast crank-angle model for vibration and misfire.

The engine and its numbers

ParameterValue
Bore × stroke86 × 86 mm, crank radius r = 43 mm
Con-rod length l; ratio λ = r/l145 mm; 0.297
Displacement1,998 cc
Firing order; crank throws1-3-4-2; 0°, 180°, 180°, 0°
Idle / redline800 / 6,000 rpm
Peak torque curveT_max(N) = 190 [1 − 0.53 ((N − 4000)/4000)²] N·m
LoadL = T_brake / T_max(N)

The slow loop (20 steps per simulated second)

  • Friction and energy: friction pressure FMEP = (97 + 15n + 5n²) · (1 + max(0, 90 − T_oil)/70) kPa with n = rpm/1000. Cold oil is thick, so friction is higher when cold. Indicated efficiency is η = 0.38 (0.70 + 0.30 L). Fuel power = brake power ÷ efficiency.
  • Coolant temperature: C_th dT/dt = (heat into coolant) − UA · (T − T_ambient). Heat into coolant is (0.34 − 0.12 L) of fuel power. The radiator UA depends on a thermostat that opens linearly from 82 to 95 °C, and on the fan, which switches on above 98 °C with hysteresis. C_th = 100 kJ/K.
  • Oil temperature: a second heat balance, warmed by friction and by the coolant, cooled by the air.
  • Oil pressure: min[ P_relief, (P_idle + K_N (N − N_idle)) · μ_rel^0.5 · H_pump / (1 + K_c W_b) ] with viscosity μ_rel = e^(−0.015 (T_oil − 90)). Cold and thick gives 2.54 bar at idle; hot idle gives 1.39 bar.
  • Battery voltage: V = V_bat + H_alt · g(N) · (V_reg − V_bat) − ΔV_load, with g(N) = 1 − e^(−N/600).
Health factorsA fault is a number between 0 and 1 that multiplies part of the physics (pump health, cooling health, cylinder combustion health). Every health factor is 1 in a healthy engine. The Twin runs the same functions with every health factor forced to 1, driven only by the measured speed, load and ambient temperature.

The sensor model

reading = true value + bias + drift(t) + noise plus: dropout (reads null, never 0 or NaN), stuck, spike

Noise levels (one standard deviation): rpm 5, load 0.004, ambient 0.1 °C, coolant 0.2 °C, oil temperature 0.3 °C, oil pressure 0.03 bar, bus voltage 0.03 V.

The fast loop: one crank angle drives everything

  • One 720° cycle is integrated in 0.5° steps. Each cylinder makes a half-sine torque pulse during its power stroke; the four pulses are offset by 180° in the order 1-3-4-2.
  • The crank speed changes as torque in exceeds the load (energy form: the kinetic energy changes by the area between the torque and the load). A governor holds the mean speed.
  • That one model produces the crank-speed ripple (about 11 rpm peak to peak, healthy, at 3,000 rpm / 80 N·m), the vibration signal (second-order force F₂ = 4 m r ω² λ cos 2θ plus combustion orders, 1× imbalance, knock impacts and noise), and what the 3D pistons do (slider-crank equation).
  • A window of 16 revolutions is sampled at 512 points per revolution (8,192 points), which is what the FFT analyses. Sampling per revolution, not per second, is called order tracking: a fault at "0.5×" stays at 0.5× at any speed.

Why the same run is always identical

Every random number comes from one seeded generator (src/lib/rng.ts). The scenarios run on the simulated clock. So the oil-pump and walkthrough scenarios play the same way on every computer.

Chapter 6

How the diagnosis works

From raw readings to a named fault, in eight small steps. Each is simple enough to explain on a whiteboard.

  1. Residual. r = measured − expected. Normalised: z = r / σ, where σ is that sensor's noise. |z| under 2 is noise; well above 2 is a real gap.
  2. Sensor sanity first. Before any reading is trusted it is checked: is the rate of change physically possible (coolant cannot heat faster than 0.81 K/s), is it stuck, has it dropped out, does the oil temperature follow the coolant? A reading that fails is left out of the diagnosis and reported as a sensor fault.
  3. Risk. Each residual becomes a 0-to-1 risk with a warning and a critical value: R = clip((x − W)/(C − W), 0, 1).
  4. Fault evidence score. For each fault, S = Σ(weight × risk) / Σ(weight). Weights, for example lubrication: 0.35 low oil pressure, 0.20 pressure residual, 0.20 oil temperature, 0.15 vibration, 0.10 pressure decay. The highest score names the fault.
  5. Health. Each subsystem gets 0–100; overall H = 100 (1 − Σ w R / Σ w). Critical overrides: oil-pressure risk above 0.95 goes straight to CRITICAL.
  6. Alert hysteresis. To avoid flicker: NORMAL to WATCH needs risk above 0.30 for 5 s; WATCH to WARNING above 0.55 for 10 s; WARNING to CRITICAL above 0.85 for 5 s; WARNING clears only below 0.40 for 20 s (all in simulated seconds).
  7. Statistics. CUSUM: S = max(0, S + z − 0.5), alarm at 5. A residual that sits at z = 1 never crosses a 3 σ limit, yet CUSUM passes 5 in about 11 samples. D²: the distance of all five residuals together from the healthy baseline learned in the first 60 s, against the χ² 99 % limit of 15.09.
  8. Remaining life. Fit a straight line to the recent health of the worst subsystem. RUL = (H − H_fail) / |slope|, with a band from the slope's standard error (±2 s_b). It is shown only if the slope is negative and significant, and it is in simulated time at the present load.

Naming the misfiring cylinder

A misfire removes one power pulse every two revolutions. That repeats at half the crank frequency (order 0.5). The cylinders fire 180° of crank apart, which is 90° of phase at half order, so each cylinder has its own phase:

CylinderFires at0.5× phase
10°+45°
3180°−45°
4360°−135°
2540°+135°

The governor keeps the mean speed, so with one cylinder out the other three must do four cylinders' work: the torque command rises by exactly 4/3 (in the app, about 111 to 148 N·m).

Proving a sensor lied

fastest possible heating = (max heat into coolant) / C_th = 81,500 W / 100,000 J/K ≈ 0.8 K/s a reading that jumps 25 °C in 0.10 s implies about 250 K/s, over 300 times the limit

The engine cannot do that, so the sensor failed. The other sensors and the Twin agree with each other, so the cooling system is not blamed.

Wording rules the app followsA weighted fault score is a "diagnostic evidence score", never a confidence percentage. Vibration thresholds are "engine-profile calibration values", never ISO limits. A crank journal-bearing fault is never labelled with rolling-bearing frequencies (BPFO/BPFI), because those formulas do not apply.

Questions visitors ask, and short answers

QuestionAnswer
Isn't it just reading the fault you injected?No. The analytics only receive telemetry; the fault lives in a module they cannot import (a lint rule enforces it). The blind test proves it.
How accurate is the model?A reduced-order model with demo calibration, checked against hand calculations on the Validation page. Absolute accuracy needs calibration on a real engine.
Why not deep learning?There is no real failure data. A network trained on our simulator would just learn our own equations back. Our statistics have a known false-alarm rate; machine learning becomes useful once real data is logged.
What is the false-alarm rate?D² uses the χ² 99 % limit, so about 1 healthy sample in 100 crosses it. An alarm needs 3 s of persistence. In our tests a healthy engine never raised a held alarm.
Why does an inline-4 shake at twice crank speed?The first-order piston forces cancel between the pairs of cylinders; the second-order forces add. That is 2,517 N peak at 3,000 rpm.
How do you know which cylinder misfired?From the phase of the half-order crank-speed component: +45°, +135°, −45°, −135° for cylinders 1, 2, 3, 4.
Why exactly 4/3?The governor holds the speed, so mean torque must still match the load. With one cylinder out, three pulses carry four pulses' worth.
Sensor spike or real overheating?Physics: the engine cannot heat faster than about 0.8 K/s, and a real rise would also warm the oil through the water jacket.
Is the RUL real?It is a trend estimate: a straight-line fit with a ±2 standard-error band, shown only when the slope is significant, and conditional on the current load.

Chapter 11

Limitations to state honestly

Saying these yourself is a strength. It shows you know what the model is and is not.

  • The telemetry is synthetic. It is not a substitute for engine-test data, and every coefficient is demo calibration.
  • The thermal model is lumped: one coolant temperature and one oil temperature, with no spatial gradients.
  • The lubrication model is an empirical pressure law, not a bearing-film (Reynolds) solver.
  • The crank model ignores compression torque and cycle-to-cycle combustion scatter. The governor is modelled at its settled state, not as a transient controller.
  • Vibration amplitudes are calibrated to look sensible, not measured. Thresholds are engine-profile calibration values, never ISO limits.
  • A slow drift in the oil-pressure or voltage sensor looks exactly like pump or alternator wear, because there is no physical cross-check for those channels. (Coolant has one: the oil must follow a real coolant rise.)
  • The remaining life is conditional on the assumed wear law and on the load continuing. Stop-and-go driving makes the trend noisy, so none is shown until it is significant.
  • The evidence scores are weighted sums, not calibrated probabilities. That is why the app never says "confidence %".
  • The overall health is a weighted average, so it can look mild beside one critical subsystem. The alert level carries the critical override.
  • ESP32 and OBD-II are not yet tested on real hardware. 60 fps depends on the laptop's graphics card.

Glossary

TermMeaning
Digital twinA live model of a physical thing, run alongside it and compared with it.
PlantIn IgniSense, the simulated "real" engine, the only place hidden faults exist.
TwinThe healthy reference model. It predicts what each sensor should read.
TelemetryThe one common data format every source produces (rpm, load, temperatures, pressure, voltage, and so on).
Residual (r, z)Measured minus expected; z is that gap in units of the sensor's noise.
Twin markerThe white tick or pointer showing the Twin's expected value on a gauge.
Health factorA 0-to-1 multiplier on part of the physics. 1 = healthy.
SeverityHow bad an injected fault is, 0 to 1.
Evidence scoreThe weighted 0-to-1 support for a fault. Not a probability.
CUSUMCumulative sum of small residuals; catches a slow persistent drift early.
D² (Mahalanobis)A single number for how unusual the whole set of residuals is compared with healthy running.
χ² limitThe threshold D² exceeds only about 1 time in 100 for a healthy engine (15.09 for 5 signals).
RULRemaining useful life: how long until health reaches the failure level, at the current trend and load.
HysteresisRequiring a condition to persist (or clear) for some time, so alerts do not flicker.
Order (0.5×, 1×, 2×)A multiple of crank speed. Order tracking keeps a fault at the same order at any rpm.
F₂The second-order reciprocating force that makes an inline-4 shake at twice crank speed.
MisfireA cylinder that fails to fire, removing one power pulse every two revolutions.
Time-warpRunning the simulated clock faster than real time (1×, 10×, 60×).
Demo calibrationA parameter chosen to give sensible behaviour, not measured on a real engine.
